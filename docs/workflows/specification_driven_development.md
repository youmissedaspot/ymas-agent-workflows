# Specification-driven development

Version: v3
Previous version: [v2](../archive/workflows/specification_driven_development_v2.md)
Change summary: Add decision worksheets, sibling SPEC/SPECARC definitions, and whole-system integration without mandatory early artifacts.

Use this workflow to formalize a True Specification conversation and settled decisions, or for substantive product or system changes when important rules, architecture, boundaries, or staged scope remain unresolved. The conceptual flow is **Discuss → True Spec Worksheet → Decide → SPEC and/or SPECARC → systems.md → Plan → Implement → Verify → Human Acceptance where applicable**. It is a workflow model, not a requirement to create every artifact. Inspect an existing project and resume at the appropriate stage; small or already-specified work bypasses unnecessary discovery and documentation. A specification-only request ends with the requested specifications and relevant map review/update, without starting implementation.

## Specification roles and project authority

| Role | Question it owns | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **True Specification discussion and worksheet** | What do users need, which decisions are settled, and what consequential product or architecture choices remain? | The worksheet is a provisional decision aid, not authoritative product truth. Recommendations and evidence remain distinct from acceptance. |
| **Accepted decisions** | Which choices has the governing owner actually accepted? | Preserve sources, scope, qualifications, and the project's acceptance process. Do not infer approval from a completed worksheet. |
| **SPEC** | What must be true for the product or system? Formalize accepted requirements and relevant detailed behavior, invariants, contracts, exceptional paths, verification, and acceptance cases. | Approved definitions govern within project-defined scope and precedence; a draft or family prefix grants no authority. |
| **SPECARC** | How is a defined architectural concern designed? Specify boundaries, component/state ownership, interfaces, flows, consistency/reconciliation, persistence, lifecycles, dependencies, integration, architectural invariants, and relevant failures. | A sibling of SPEC, not a delivery plan. Architecture can be specified before all surrounding product specifications or the integrated map exist. |
| **Integrated architecture/system map (`systems.md`)** | How do the complete current specifications form one coherent system through responsibilities, ownership, interfaces, dependencies, flows, lifecycles, and enforcing authorities? | Integrates SPEC and SPECARC; it is not the mandatory first architecture-design artifact and cannot invent or override accepted requirements. |
| **Milestone / increment scope** | Which subset of already specified systems is in this increment? Reference higher authority, dependencies, entry/exit conditions, deferrals, and acceptance criteria. | Scope selection only; never redefine product or system truth. Planning labels stay in plans and history, not durable production names. |

SPEC and SPECARC have no fixed order relative to one another. An accepted event-reconciliation mechanism, authoritative state model, replay behavior, or interface boundary may become SPECARC before surrounding SPEC documents are complete, provided it respects governing authority. Neither family automatically outranks the other. The adopting repository defines approval and precedence and may map these roles onto established documents; this package does not impose a universal hierarchy or new filenames on an unrelated project.

ADR records a specific architecture decision with context and rationale where adopted; it may support SPECARC but is not automatically interchangeable with it. SYS retains its existing system-level documentation role where adopted. `systems.md` is the complete-set integration map, distinct from both. Preserve established SYS owners and IDs; do not rename them into SPECARC or allocate an ID merely to version an unnumbered map. Conversations and worksheets supply source material; approved specifications define their subjects under project authority. Surface consequential gaps and conflicts to the owner.

In every layer, label the governing authority, accepted decisions, unresolved questions, proposals, recommendations, implementation evidence, and verification results when they appear. Evidence and recommendations do not become product truth without the project's acceptance process.

## From conversation and decisions to specifications and systems.md

1. **Discuss and settle consequential decisions.** Inspect relevant current authority, specifications, accepted decisions, operational context, and an existing `systems.md` for architectural context and affected dependencies. When unresolved choices warrant structure, use [true-spec-worksheet](../../skills/true-spec-worksheet/SKILL.md) to prepare questions, recommendations, reasoning, meaningful alternatives, downstream consequences, and decision capture in dependency order. Do not reopen settled decisions without cause. Undefined consequential choices and conflicting authority go to the project's decision owner. Skip the worksheet when the relevant decisions are already settled.
2. **Formalize accepted decisions into SPEC and/or SPECARC.** Reuse existing owners; create only the specification types needed, in the order the design requires. Trace consequential rules and architectural choices to explicit acceptance sources. Use the [architecture template](../../skills/spec-driven-development/assets/architecture_specification_template.md) for a defined concern when useful. Keep proposals and unresolved choices visibly provisional. Read an existing map for architecture and impact context, but do not let it override newly accepted higher product decisions or invent requirements to preserve old architecture. Its absence does not block specification work.
3. **Review the complete current set for integration.** After substantive SPEC or SPECARC creation or revision, inventory and review the **entire current specification set**, including unaffected documents and both sibling types. Distinguish accepted requirements from drafts and archives. Record each specification's stable ID/path, version (or unversioned status), acceptance status, and mapped systems. Create or update `systems.md` when the set supports a useful integrated architecture; an early isolated concern need not produce a meaningless map. Record that deferral and the integration gaps rather than claim a complete architecture. Use the [systems map checklist](../../skills/spec-driven-development/references/systems_map_checklist.md) and [template](../../skills/spec-driven-development/assets/systems_map_template.md). Preserve an existing map's path; for a new map use the project's established directory (default `docs/systems/systems.md`).
4. **Map the whole architecture and its relationships.** Identify major systems, governing specifications, responsibilities, state ownership, cross-boundary reads/writes, communication, interfaces, upstream/downstream dependencies, integrations, lifecycles, and cross-system invariants with their enforcing authorities. Describe data, commands, events, resources, and state crossing boundaries. Map specifications to systems and one another through shared contracts, constraints, dependencies, and overlaps. Link SPECARC for concern-level architecture detail; the map must integrate the whole set rather than duplicate one concern or list documents without relationships.
5. **Check coverage and consistency.** Account for every current specification and trace each mapped system and relationship to its sources. Flag conflicts, unmapped requirements, unresolved ownership, and proposed architecture for the governing owner; continue independent settled work. If mapping reveals a missing decision, return it to the conversation or owning specification, then refresh the map. The map cannot silently override accepted specification rules. Do not claim the map complete while consequential coverage gaps remain.

Version both the specifications and `systems.md` using the project's standard and [document versioning](document_versioning.md). A new document starts at `v1`; revising the same document preserves its sequence ID and current path, writes the newer version there, and archives the complete predecessor. Record the resulting versions and archive paths in the handoff. Sequence numbers and increment labels are never document versions.

After substantive SPEC or SPECARC creation or revision, review the complete current specification set and update `systems.md` when the change affects architecture, ownership, boundaries, dependencies, interfaces, flows, or cross-system behavior. For a change that leaves an existing map accurate, report the review without mechanically bumping its version. A meaningful map may remain incomplete with explicitly tracked gaps; do not invent decisions to complete it. Check whether the total architecture implied by the set forms a coherent system.

## Transition to implementation

6. **Inspect repository reality.** After the relevant specification layers are sufficiently settled, inspect current architecture, implementation, tests, schemas, interfaces, and unusual behavior's relevant Git history. Compare them with authority; history and code are evidence, not product authority. Earlier exploratory inspection may inform design without silently settling requirements.
7. **Plan.** Translate the specifications into affected components, order, ownership boundaries, migrations, tests, risks, integration points, justified compatibility needs, and exclusions. If this exposes a product gap, return to its specification owner.
8. **Review adversarially when complexity warrants.** Challenge the plan for missing requirements, contradictions, authority violations, hidden coupling, incorrect ownership, failure and concurrency gaps, migrations, security, tests, naming leaks, unjustified compatibility, and divergence from intent. A reviewer may identify gaps, not invent product decisions. Use the [plan review rubric](../../skills/spec-driven-development/references/implementation_plan_review.md).
9. **Implement and verify.** Use bounded units, the [execution loop](agent_execution_loop.md), and isolated workbranches. Inspect before editing, verify each meaningful unit, repair the smallest failed unit, and reassess after three failures on that unit. Never relax accepted product rules merely because implementation is difficult.
10. **Prepare human acceptance.** Agent tests, code review, logs, schemas, and API exercises provide verification evidence. Where the project reserves final product acceptance to a human owner, prepare a concise [manual checklist](../../skills/spec-driven-development/references/human_acceptance_checklist.md) and leave sign-off to that owner unless the repository establishes another authority model.

If detailed design or planning reveals an earlier error, repair the smallest incorrect authoritative unit, propagate the correction to dependent layers, and rerun affected review. Do not rewrite unrelated settled work or force progress through a known bad assumption.
